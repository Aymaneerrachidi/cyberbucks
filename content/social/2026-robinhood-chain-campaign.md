# CyberBucks x Robinhood Chain — social campaign

## Creative position

CyberBucks should not compete with Robinhood Chain memecoins by pretending to be the newest, loudest animal. Its distinctive angle is the opposite: the name comes with real digital-cash history from 1994, including a public DigiCash trial, free starter balances, blind-signature privacy, software wallets, micropayments, and an eccentric web merchant economy.

The recurring idea is: **before Bitcoin, there was CyberBucks. Now the story comes alive on Robinhood Chain.** The humor comes from digital currency history colliding with today's meme economy.

All ten images deliberately contain essentially no marketing copy. Put the hook, facts, contract details, and disclaimer in the post caption—not inside the art.

## Research summary

- Robinhood Chain public mainnet launched July 1, 2026. Robinhood describes it as an Ethereum Layer 2 built using Arbitrum technology, with a permissionless builder environment and day-one integrations including Uniswap.
- Official network documentation lists mainnet chain ID 4663 and ETH as the native gas token.
- The chain was positioned around tokenized real-world assets, but memes became a major early source of activity. CoinGecko calculated that memecoin trades represented 79.2% of DEX volume on July 27, 2026 under its stated classification method.
- CASHCAT became the breakout early meme. Contemporary reporting also identified Cash Dog in Hood, Little John, Hoodrat, and Arrow among the Robinhood-themed wave.
- Launchpad activity was extremely fast and unstable. CoinGecko reported that Noxa deployed close to 60,000 tokens during its active window before pausing launches; Pons.family then gained share. Treat launchpad rankings and token-market figures as snapshots, never evergreen copy.
- A particularly native Robinhood Chain behavior is the pairing of memecoins directly with tokenized stocks, including meme-stock and themed pairs.
- Vlad Tenev publicly wrote that although the chain was being built for RWAs, it also “works great for memes too.” Any art featuring him must be posted as parody/editorial commentary and must not imply endorsement.
- David Chaum published the blind-signature paper in 1982. DigiCash later launched eCash online under the CyberBucks trial currency name in 1994.
- A 1995 W3 conference paper described an eCash trial with about 10,000 participants, each receiving 100 CyberBucks free, and sample web shops where they could spend them.
- A 1995 European Commission notice reported that DigiCash had issued one million CyberBucks to Internet users.
- The eCash client stored coins locally, enabling small payments without contacting the bank for every purchase. Preserved interfaces include a two-cent tic-tac-toe payment request, and the historical merchant archive includes books, software, digital art, shirts, postcards, games, exchange services, and a web wishing well.

## Recommended posting order

1. Start with 02 to establish the 1994 discovery hook without leaning on a public figure.
2. Follow with 03 while Robinhood Chain's “RWA versus memes” story is current.
3. Post 05 and 06 as a two-part “things that really happened” mini-series.
4. Use 01 as a high-reach parody post, with the independence disclaimer in the same post.
5. Run 04 as the clean brand reset.
6. Use 07 and 08 for deeper lore.
7. Finish with 09 and 10 as reusable community-reaction memes.

## Posting guardrails

- Say **planned for**, **building for**, or **coming to** Robinhood Chain until deployment is complete and independently verifiable.
- Never write or visually imply that Robinhood, Vlad Tenev, DigiCash, or David Chaum sponsors or endorses the current project.
- Do not call the current token the same financial instrument as the 1994 DigiCash trial currency. Say it carries the name, story, or digital-cash spark forward.
- Keep prices, TVL, DEX volume, rankings, and launchpad share dated and sourced. They change quickly.
- Avoid profit promises, “guaranteed,” “safe,” or price-target copy.
- Add the contract address only after verifying it from the project's official deployment source; do not bake it into generated art.
